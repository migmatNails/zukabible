# ZUKA — APPENDIX F: MASTER CAPITAL REGISTRY

> Source: Volume II, Ch.17 | ADR-005 | Zuka-Bible 2, Book I Part A, Ch.3.5

Capital Ontology is constitutional (ADR-005). Seven types. No competing taxonomy
may be introduced without a constitutional amendment.

| Capital | Definition | Primary Source | Primary Consumer | Moat Relevance |
|---------|-----------|---------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Memory Capital | Accumulated preserved meaning across a person's participation history | Memory domain (Gatherings → Check-in → Memory creation) | Personal Twin, Memory Historian, Anniversary Engine | HIGH — appreciates over time; irreplaceable if context lost |
| Relationship Capital | Depth and breadth of social connections | Relationship entities; co-attendance at Gatherings | Opportunity Agent (matching), Trust Graph | HIGH — network effects compound |
| Trust Capital | Earned confidence across six dimensions | Trust domain; Trust Timeline | Opportunity emergence; Creator Economy matching | CRITICAL — primary prerequisite for all Opportunity |
| Community Capital | Standing within and across communities | Community membership; CommunityTrustProfile | Community Intelligence Layer; Tradition Engine | HIGH — communities outlive individuals |
| Experience Capital | Breadth and depth of gathering experience | Gathering attendance; Tradition participation | Recommendation systems; City Pulse | MODERATE — cross-cutting, hard to attribute to one stage |
| Opportunity Capital | Value-creation pathways earned through trust + community | Opportunity domain; completed bookings/jobs | Creator Economy; Sponsorship Engine | MODERATE — direct economic value |
| Legacy Capital | What persists beyond active participation | Memory Vault; Community Archive; Tradition stewardship | Legacy Historian; Memorial records | HIGH — the civilisation-scale measure |

**Capital generation chain:**
Participation → Experience + Memory → Relationships → Community → Trust → Opportunity → Economies → Legacy

All seven capitals accumulate. None are transactional. Once lost, some (Memory, Legacy) are
permanently irretrievable — which is why preservation is always the default posture.
