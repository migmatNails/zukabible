## Constitutional Premise

A community is a persistent network of relationships organised around shared
participation. (Zuka-Bible 2, Book I Part B, Ch.7.1)

Communities are discovered, not manufactured. Communities outlive individuals.
A community's memory is not the sum of its members' individual memories.

## The Eight Community Families (Volume VI, Ch.5)

```
Family           — biological and chosen family units
Faith            — religious, spiritual, and worship communities
Educational      — schools, universities, alumni networks, study groups
Geographic       — neighbourhood, district, city-level communities
Professional     — industry, trade, career-based communities
Interest         — hobby, sport, art, culture-based communities
Cultural         — ethnic, linguistic, traditional heritage communities
Event            — communities that form around recurring gatherings
```

Each family has distinct governance patterns, succession models, and trust profiles,
but all share the same constitutional Community entity structure.

## Community Lifecycle (Volume VI, Ch.22) — Five Stages

```
Formation      — small group, informal, trust establishing
Growth         — expanding membership, governance formalising
Maturity       — institutionalisation of practices and traditions
Transformation — leadership succession, restructuring, or difficult periods
               (this stage is real and must never be omitted from the model)
Legacy         — multi-generational continuity; community has outlived
               its founding members
```

The four-stage summary (Formation → Growth → Institution → Legacy) from
Zuka-Bible 2 is valid for external communication. The five-stage model
including Transformation is the operative specification.

## Community Governance Model (Volume VI, Part XIV)

Four governance layers, explicitly decoupled:

```
OPERATIONAL LAYER   — day-to-day decisions (event scheduling, routine comms)
                      Authority: any active leadership role holder
FINANCIAL LAYER     — budget, treasury, fundraising decisions
                      Authority: Treasurer role + governance consensus
HISTORICAL LAYER    — Archive decisions; what is preserved and how
                      Authority: Archivist role (sole constitutional owner)
STRATEGIC LAYER     — governance model changes, federation, dissolution,
                      Twin/AI consent, major policy changes
                      Authority: community-wide ratification per governance_model
```

Decision models: `elder_consensus` | `founder_authority` | `membership_election`
| `committee_vote` | `archivist_authority` (historical layer only)

## Community Trust Profile (Volume VI, Part XI)

Six dimensions — parallel to but distinct from individual TrustProfile:

```
community_reliability      — does it show up and deliver on what it promises?
community_authenticity     — is it what it claims to be?
community_safety           — do members feel physically and psychologically safe?
community_inclusion        — does it welcome appropriately diverse membership?
community_continuity       — has it demonstrated longevity and stability?
community_stewardship      — does leadership care for members' long-term interests?
```

Trust_inheritance: when a person joins a community with a high trust profile,
a portion of that profile's composite score is temporarily inherited (not permanently
transferred) as a baseline signal for new relationship edges (Volume VI, Ch.21).

## Community Succession (Volume VI, Part XXII)

The most important architectural guarantee in this domain:
*"Communities outlive individuals."*

Succession is explicitly DECOUPLED — four independent succession chains:
```
Leadership succession    → governance model's own process
Archive stewardship      → Archivist role transfer
Financial custody        → Treasurer role transfer  
Tradition stewardship    → passed to specific named stewards per tradition
```

No single event (a founder's death, a leadership dispute) triggers an all-or-nothing
community succession. Each chain resolves independently.

## Community Federation (Volume VI, Part XIX)

Communities may federate — forming parent/child relationships:

```
Parent community    → sets federation policy, may set shared governance rules
Child community     → retains own identity, governance, and Archive
Federation edge     → FederationRelationship entity (Appendix A)
Trust inheritance   → child community may inherit a floor from parent's profile
```
