## Chapter 1 — The Constitutional Premise

Trust is the invisible infrastructure of human coordination.

```
Identity creates participation.
Participation creates trust.
Trust creates opportunities.
Opportunities create economies.
Without trust, the ecosystem collapses.
```

### The Four Trust Principles

```
EARNED       Trust is earned through behaviour, never through claims or payment.
EXPLAINABLE  Every trust outcome answers: Why? Based on what signals? Over what
             period? At what confidence? (ProvenanceRecord mandatory.)
CONTEXTUAL   Trust is domain-specific (event trust ≠ financial trust ≠
             leadership trust). Six dimensions, never one number.
TEMPORAL     Trust is a journey. Trust history matters more than trust state.
             Trust may decay and recover.
```

## Chapter 3 — The Six Trust Dimensions

```
reliability         Does the person do what they commit to?
authenticity        Are they who they say they are?
professionalism     Do they meet the standards of their role?
safety              Do others feel safe in their presence?
consistency         Is their behaviour stable over time?
community_standing  How do communities regard them?
```

These six are never collapsed into a single exposed number. (Trust Visibility
resolution: internal richness, external human-readable states only.)

## Chapter 7 — Trust Decay (research-grounded)

Drawing on current trust-systems research (time-decayed weighting, behavioural
consistency monitoring):

```
Trust is not permanent. Long inactivity reduces confidence.
Decay must be: Explainable · Gradual · Reversible.

Mechanism: time-decayed weighting of trust events. Recent consistent behaviour
weighs more than old behaviour; a long-dormant account's trust confidence
gradually decreases (not its history — its current confidence). This prevents
"sleeper" accounts from banking trust and exploiting it later, mirroring the
time-decayed stake-weighting and reputation-aging mechanisms proven in
contemporary Sybil-resistant systems.
```

## Chapter 8 — Trust Inheritance (bounded)

```
Partial trust transfer, never absolute:
  Trusted Community → New Subcommunity (inherits a floor, not the full score)
  Trusted Organisation → New Initiative

A new member of a high-trust community inherits a temporary baseline signal —
never a permanent transfer. Trust is always ultimately earned per-entity.
```

## Chapter 9 — Anti-Fraud Architecture (this Volume embodies the Crown Jewel IP)

```
THREAT TAXONOMY:
  Identity Fraud      Fake accounts · farms · synthetic identities · takeovers
  Ticketing Fraud     Duplicate tickets · QR abuse · resale · chargeback abuse
  Community Fraud     Fake communities · governance manipulation · capture
  Trust Fraud         Fake reviews · review rings · endorsement abuse · farming
  Opportunity Fraud   Referral · sponsorship · scholarship abuse

FOUR SIGNAL TYPES (the shared detection substrate):
  device_clustering        Multiple accounts, one device fingerprint
  graph_topology_anomaly   Dense reciprocal low-diversity sub-graphs (Sybil rings)
  velocity_anomaly         Abnormal action rates
  content_pattern_match    Coordinated review/content patterns

The Fraud Graph operates on nodes (Person, Device, Payment, Community, Event)
and edges (ATTENDED, PAID_WITH, REFERRED, REVIEWED, ENDORSED). Detection is
graph-pattern-based, drawing on behavioural-consistency monitoring research.
```

## Chapter 10 — The Sybil Defense Framework

```
Signals: Device Fingerprints · Session Similarity · Network Similarity ·
         Relationship Density · Behavioral Patterns

NO single signal determines guilt. Verified identity (L2+) is the primary
analogue defence — linking accounts to verified humans makes large-scale
Sybil attacks impractical, the same principle centralized marketplaces use
via payment-instrument linking.
```

## Chapter 11 — Enforcement Ladder (Progressive, ADR-014)

```
L0 Observe → L1 Soft Warning → L2 Reduced Visibility →
L3 Capability Restrictions → L4 Temporary Suspension → L5 Permanent Ban

Never jump to maximum punishment without justification.
Fraud systems DETECT and FLAG. Only humans ACT. (ADR-014 — revocation is
always a human act.) No automated permanent ban, ever.
```

---

## Chapter 12 — The Appeals Mechanism (due process)

```
Four steps:
  1. View Explanation   (the ProvenanceRecord behind the decision)
  2. Appeal             (submit grounds)
  3. Human Review       (a person evaluates)
  4. Append-Only Correction  (a new event corrects; the original is never edited)

Every enforcement action carries: Reason · Evidence · Confidence · Appeal Path.
Trust without appeals is forbidden.
```
