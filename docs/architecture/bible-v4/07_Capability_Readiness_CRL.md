## Chapter 1 — The Core Discovery

```
DRL answers:  Can a capability EXIST?       (Is the data honest enough?)
CRL answers:  How DEEPLY should it manifest? (Is the relationship mature enough?)
```

Two independent axes. Both must clear before any output is permitted.

---

## Chapter 2 — The Governing Inequality

```
manifestation_depth ≤ min(DRL_permits, CRL_earned)
```

A capability manifests at the shallower of the two limits. High data maturity with a
shallow relationship still produces only shallow output, and vice versa. All entities
start at CRL: Observing. No head starts.

## Chapter 5 — Geographic Normalisation (anti-urban-bias)

CRL must never simply reward the biggest city. Maturity is measured by density and
quality, not raw volume:

```
Use:    Participation Density · Relationship Density · Trust Density · Continuity
Avoid:  Raw volume comparisons

A small town with deep, consistent, high-trust participation may have a higher
CRL than a large city with shallow, churning participation. This is the
Founder Rule: fairness through density, not size.
```

## Chapter 7 — Adaptive UX (the experience translation)

Same capability, different depth, never exposed as a number:

```
New user:       "Beginning Your Story"
Advanced user:  "Your Ten-Year Journey"

NEVER shown:    CRL = 4
ALWAYS shown:   "Trusted Contributor" · "Community Steward" ·
                "Established Organiser"
```

This is the Growth-Not-Exclusion principle (Volume 01, Principle 3) made operational.
Users experience capability as growing with them, never as abruptly locking or
unlocking.
