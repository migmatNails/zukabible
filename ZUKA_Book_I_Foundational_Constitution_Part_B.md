# ZUKA Unified Architecture Bible v3.0
# BOOK I — FOUNDATIONAL CONSTITUTION
## PART B — PHILOSOPHY

---

# Chapter 5 — Reality Doctrine

## 5.1 Constitutional Principle
Reality precedes data.
Data precedes knowledge.
Knowledge precedes intelligence.

## 5.2 Primacy of Reality
No model, algorithm, prediction, AI system, reputation system, or recommendation engine may supersede reality.

## 5.3 Observed Reality
ZUKA shall prioritize observed participation over declared participation.

## 5.4 Historical Reality
Historical events must preserve continuity and provenance.

## 5.5 Reality Before AI
Artificial Intelligence may interpret reality.
Artificial Intelligence may not redefine reality.

---

# Chapter 6 — Participation Doctrine

## 6.1 Participation as the Source of Value
All sustainable value within ZUKA originates from participation.

## 6.2 Forms of Participation
- Attendance
- Contribution
- Organization
- Mentorship
- Stewardship
- Collaboration
- Creation

## 6.3 Participation Hierarchy
Presence → Engagement → Contribution → Stewardship → Legacy

## 6.4 Participation Integrity
Participation records must be resistant to manipulation and fraud.

## 6.5 Participation Continuity
Participation should accumulate meaning over time.

---

# Chapter 7 — Community Doctrine

## 7.1 Constitutional Definition
A community is a persistent network of relationships organized around shared participation.

## 7.2 Community Principles
- Communities are discovered, not manufactured.
- Communities are strengthened through participation.
- Communities generate trust.

## 7.3 Community Health
Community success is measured through:
- Continuity
- Participation
- Trust
- Stewardship
- Resilience

## 7.4 Community Sovereignty
Communities retain identity independent of platform mechanics.

## 7.5 Community Legacy
Communities may persist across generations.

---

# Chapter 8 — Trust Doctrine

## 8.1 Constitutional Definition
Trust is confidence established through observed participation and historical behavior.

## 8.2 Trust Principles
- Trust cannot be purchased.
- Trust cannot be inherited automatically.
- Trust must be earned.
- Trust may decay.
- Trust may recover.

## 8.3 Trust Sources
- Participation
- Reliability
- Stewardship
- Community Recognition
- Historical Consistency

## 8.4 Trust Protection
Trust systems must actively resist fraud, manipulation and artificial inflation.

## 8.5 Trust Transparency
Users should understand trust outcomes without exposure to internal scoring complexity.

---

# Chapter 9 — Memory Doctrine

## 9.1 Constitutional Definition
Memory is preserved meaning derived from participation.

## 9.2 Memory Principles
- Media is not memory.
- Memory contains context.
- Memory creates continuity.
- Memory contributes to identity.

## 9.3 Memory Lifecycle
Capture → Preservation → Interpretation → Discovery → Legacy

## 9.4 Memory Preservation
Meaningful memories should be preserved where governance permits.

## 9.5 Memory and Legacy
Memories form the foundation of individual and collective legacy.

---

# Chapter 10 — Opportunity Doctrine

## 10.1 Constitutional Definition
Opportunity is the possibility of value creation emerging from trust, participation, relationships, and communities.

## 10.2 Opportunity Principles
- Opportunity emerges from participation.
- Opportunity depends on trust.
- Opportunity strengthens communities.
- Opportunity creates prosperity.

## 10.3 Opportunity Types
- Social
- Educational
- Professional
- Economic
- Civic
- Cultural

## 10.4 Opportunity Discovery
The platform should help surface relevant opportunities without compromising trust.

## 10.5 Opportunity Equity
Discovery systems should maximize fairness, merit, relevance, and contribution.
